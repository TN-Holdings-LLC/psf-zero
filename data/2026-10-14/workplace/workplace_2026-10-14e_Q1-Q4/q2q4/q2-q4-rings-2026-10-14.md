# Q2 and Q4 (2026-10-14): rings by N2's formula in the compiler (item 92) are not shorter than c53; a constant-depth ring schedule for any n helps the AI front end (a17 item 31)

**Status: Q2 stopped after the feasibility check, as the common rule asks.** Item 92 was built and is exact, but it
loses to c53 for n ≥ 6. It is **not** in the recommended patch (`make_c53_91.py`); its code and builder are kept
here as a record. Q4 produced a new ring schedule, which a17 uses.

## 1. Q2: item 92 (periodic chains by N2's folded line and mirrored rounds)

**Construction** (`c53_92_code.py`, built with `make_c53_91_92.py`):

- the cycle in folded order on a line placed by item 87's rule;
- item 87's levels;
- for each level with two-qubit terms, the ring's rounds: terms on the edges made adjacent, then the round's SWAPs;
- the next level mirrored;
- single-qubit terms afterwards, then CZ (item 87) or rzz with the Pauli frame (item 88, where a SWAP carries the
  frame bits);
- comparison with c53 by item 87's rule.

Every output was exact (total variation distance ≤ 7e-15).

**Why c53 wins.**

- In a periodic Hamiltonian, the ZZ (or XX, YY) terms of one level commute. c53's STEINER6 and item 83 then build the
  closing edge, and any distance-2 edge, with CX parity gadgets on a path, which needs no SWAP.
- N2's formula moves qubits instead. A SWAP is 3 CZ, so this loses.
- The classifier's CZ ring (N2, a16) is different. There, item 73 had to read a Clifford ring between non-commuting
  rotation layers, and it did not (N3).

**Recommendation:** leave periodic Hamiltonians to c53. Item 91 already takes the 12-ring, through a hexagon.

## 2. Q4: a ring schedule for any length (CZ-depth 9, about 3n CZ per layer)

N2's rounds use odd-even transposition until every ring edge has met. That costs:

| n | 5 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 11 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CZ per layer | 17 | 55 | 38 | 117 | 203 |

**New schedule.**

- **Layout.** Positions hold 0, n−1, 1, n−2, …, so ring indices 0, 1, 2, … sit on even positions and n−1, n−2, … on
  odd positions. Every ring edge is then at distance ≤ 2.
- **Round 1.** The edges already adjacent (the two ends and the middle). Then SWAPs SA = (1,2), (5,6), (9,10), ….
- **Round 2.** The edges now adjacent. Then SA undone together with SB = (3,4), (7,8), …. SA and SB are disjoint, so
  this is one layer.
- **Round 3.** The rest.
- The next layer is mirrored.

It covers every ring with n = 4-199 (checked) in 3 rounds and 2 SWAP layers:

| n | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CZ per layer, new | 7 | 14 | 15 | 22 | 23 | 27 | 28 | 35 | 36 | 49 |
| CZ per layer, N2 | 7 | 17 | 15 | 55 | 38 | 117 | 73 | 203 | 120 | — |
| CZ-depth, new | 5 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 9 |

The lower bound from section 1 of N2 is n + 2 CZ per layer. The new schedule is about 3n.

**How a17 uses it.** a17 builds both schedules as candidates, "ring" (N2) and "ring2" (new), and item 25 takes the
shorter (item 31):

- at n = 6, N2's has the same CZ count with less waiting;
- at n = 5, a construction or the search is still shorter;
- at n = 7 and 8, the new schedule is 0.79-0.92× the best other candidate.

a17's constructed path covers n ≤ SMALL_MAX_QUBITS = 8. The W3 cells (n = 4, 6) are unchanged.
