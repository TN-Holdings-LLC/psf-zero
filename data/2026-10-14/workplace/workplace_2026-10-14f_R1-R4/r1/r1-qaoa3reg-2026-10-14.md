## 2. Fixed placements by formula (`r1_place.py`)

None of these came near. Each takes the hand-layered circuit, an initial layout given by the rule, and Qiskit level 3
with seed 0 routing:

| placement | qaoa_n12_p2 T | qaoa_n20_p2 T | qaoa3reg_n20_r3 T | (F similar) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| first Hamiltonian path (DFS from 0, neighbours in index order) on item 87's line | 11.08 | 11.26 | 20.88 | |
| logical index order on the line | 9.11 | 17.17 | 25.75 | |
| reverse Cuthill-McKee order on the line (bandwidth 6-7) | 10.10 | 11.71 | 20.72 | |
| reverse Cuthill-McKee order on a compact BFS patch | 8.54 | 20.53 | 24.85 | |
| c54 | 5.98 | 6.31 | 11.08 | |

These are 1.4-3.3× c54. A random 3-regular graph does not embed in heavy-hex (degree 3 everywhere, cycles shorter than
12), and a line or patch order leaves bandwidth 6-7. Every chord then costs SWAPs that SabreLayout's own trials avoid.

Plan B (each colour, a perfect matching, run through a linear SWAP network) was not built. The same argument as Q2
applies, and more strongly: commuting ZZ terms on non-adjacent qubits are cheaper as CX parity gadgets than as SWAPs.

**I found no formula that would reach the seed-searched numbers.**

## 3. Item 94 (optional): commuting ZZ runs re-layered by edge colouring (`make_c54_94.py`)

**What it does.**

- It applies on the outermost call.
- It reads the input's maximal runs of pairwise commuting ZZ terms (items 72-73's reader) and regroups them into layers
  of disjoint pairs by a first-fit edge colouring in their order. The layers are written as rzz; other terms are left
  unchanged. This is exact.
- c54 compiles the rewrite too. Item 84's rule takes the shorter, provided item 93's check against the input does not
  refuse it.
- Two fixed inputs, chosen by duration (as a15's constructions); no seed or placement is searched.

**Results on the family map's tests (22 cells, `r1_rows.jsonl`):**

- never longer than c54;
- shorter in 6 cells: tfim_n12 T 0.83, tfimP_n16 T 0.83, qaoa_n12_p2 T 0.93 (5.53 µs, = the user's seed 0), qaoa_n20_p2
  F 0.97, and ba_n14 F 0.998;
- unchanged elsewhere;
- all exact where checkable.

**Tests:** 7 pass (`test_94.py`). They check that the rewrite is exact, that the output is never longer than c54 and is
exact, that qaoa_n12_p2 is shorter on T, that a non-Pauli circuit and the switch-off case are identical to c54, and that
the output is deterministic.

**Caution: c54 is sensitive to the qubit order of a symmetric rzz** (`sens.py`). The same hand-layered circuit, with
each rzz's two qubits written in the other order, gives:

| test | target | order A | order B |
|---|---|---|---|
| qaoa_n12_p2 | F | 4.07 µs | 4.85 µs |
| qaoa_n20_p2 | T | 5.94 µs | 6.31 µs |
| qaoa_n20_p2 | F | 5.03 µs | 4.88 µs |

Family_map's hand-layered circuit writes pairs larger-index first (order A). Item 94 sorts them (order B), so it does
not reproduce the 4.07 on qaoa_n12_p2 F.

So part of any gain here is that sensitivity: the placement inside c54 (SabreLayout in the usual path and in item 80's
level-3 candidate) follows the DAG's node order. Taking the better of the two orders would be a search, so I did not.

**Recommendation:**

- Adopt item 94 only if home accepts "two fixed inputs, chosen by duration" as not a search. Its gains are real but
  small.
- Treat the 3-regular QAOA gap as the price of the no-search rule. Report PSF-Zero against the user's single run, or
  report the user's best as "with seed search".
