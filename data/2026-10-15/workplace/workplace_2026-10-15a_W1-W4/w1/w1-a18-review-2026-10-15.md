## 2. Concerns, most important first

**A. The hand-off's exactness check can hang on inputs that hold one large PauliEvolutionGate or QAOA gate**
(`w1_exact_time.py`).

- `_hand_off` calls `_exact(qc, out)`. That builds `Statevector(input)` from the input as written.
- A PauliEvolutionGate is then turned into a matrix by `to_matrix`, which runs a sparse `expm` on a 2^n x 2^n matrix.
- chem_n12_r1 on FakeFez T:
  - compile: 0.4 s;
  - item 30's check on the input unrolled first: 0.2 s, exact;
  - item 30's check as a18 calls it: **960 s, and the verdict is "not exact".**
- **The verdict is also wrong.** `to_matrix` gives the exact exponential exp(-iHt). The compiled circuit implements the
  gate's definition, its product formula, as psf_compile and every compiler do. So the check compares against a
  different unitary.
  - a18 then warns that c57's output is inexact, and counts it in `handoff_inexact`. The output is returned
    unchanged, so the circuit itself is not affected.
- qaoa_n12_p2: the check on the unrolled input took 0.1 s and was exact. I stopped the probe before a18's own call
  finished, to free the machine.
- At 16-20 qubits (heis2d_n16_r1, qaoa3reg_n20_r3, Benchpress' HamLib inputs) the check would not finish, or would run
  out of memory.
- c57's item 93 does not have this problem, because it unrolls the input first (product formula).
- **Proposal:** unroll the input before `_outcomes` (as item 93 does, or with `qc.decompose()` until no
  PauliEvolutionGate is left). Alternatively, reuse item 93's verdict, since `compile_for_hardware` has already checked
  the same output.

**B. Two tolerances.**

- a18's item 30 accepts a total variation distance up to 1e-9. c57's item 93 accepts a largest outcome difference up
  to 1e-6.
- R2 found c57 outputs about 1.5e-8 to 3.2e-7 away (tfim_n20_r3). So a18 would warn about outputs that c57 accepted.
- **Proposal:** use one tolerance, or say in the docstring why they differ.

**C. The ring path stops at SMALL_MAX_QUBITS = 8.**

- A ring classifier on 9 or more qubits is handed off.
- On heavy-hex, rings of 9-11 qubits do not embed (the shortest cycle is 12), so they go to Qiskit level 3. Even rings
  of 12 or more embed and get c57's path, not N2's formula.
- **Proposal:** give the ring path its own limit (for example RING_MAX_QUBITS = 20, the limit of item 30's check), so
  that item 28's formula also covers larger rings.

**D. The hand-off passes a18's own defaults, not psf_compile's.**

- With a Target, `_hand_off` calls `compile_for_hardware` with `entangling_basis="cx"` and `layout_search=True`
  (a18's defaults). psf_compile's own defaults are `"canonical"` and `False`.
- This matches how C57-VAL calls c57, so nothing changes in practice. The docstring says "psf_compile's default call",
  which is not literally true.

**E. `seed_transpiler` in kwargs raises.**

- `_hand_off` (and a16's `device_call`) pass `seed_transpiler=seed, **kwargs`.
- A caller who passes `seed_transpiler=` gets a TypeError (a keyword given twice). Other `**kwargs` are passed on
  unchecked.

**F. Smaller points.**

- The ring reader accepts only CZ rings. A classifier ring written with cx or rzz is handed off.
- Item 30 checks a measured input at up to 20 active qubits, and an unmeasured input only at up to 8.
- While HAND_OFF = True, the old a14 branch after it cannot be reached. It is kept for HAND_OFF = False, which is
  fine.
